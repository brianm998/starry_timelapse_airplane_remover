Your timelapse,
without the airplanes.

Star automatically detects and removes airplane and satellite streaks from overnight timelapse sequences — aligning neighboring frames and median-merging them so that anything which appears in only one frame simply disappears. No hand-masking. No manual review required.

HOW IT WORKS
Step 1 — Align & Combine
Neighboring frames, median-merged
Star aligns each frame to its temporal neighbors using custom OpenCV-based code. Before alignment, a horizon is detected so that foreground terrain stays fixed while only the sky region tracks star movement — preventing ground detail from smearing across the merge. The aligned neighbors are then combined per-pixel using a statistical median that discards outliers. Airplanes and satellites appear in only one frame, so they vanish completely from the merged result. This approach is standard in deep-field astrophotography and increasingly applied to wide-field timelapse work.
Automatic mode
Step 2 — Replace
Streaks replaced directly from the merged frame
The median-merged image is used directly to paint over any pixel group that doesn't belong. No manual review required — the classifier runs on every candidate group and the cleaned frames are written out immediately, making it practical to process an entire night's shoot unattended.
Selective mode
Step 2 — Classify & Paint
Subtract, classify, then correct
The merged frame is subtracted from the original to isolate brightness outliers. An ML decision tree ensemble scores each pixel group by size, brightness, and Hough-transform linearity to distinguish streaks from clouds, terrain, or meteors. Confirmed streaks are painted with pixels from the merged neighbor frame. Open the result in Star App to flip any individual group's decision before the final render.
Auto-Selective mode
Optional — Restore
Keep exactly what you want
Run the automatic pass first to get a fully clean result, then use Star App to paint back specific groups from the original frame. The selection is additive and intentional — instead of telling Star what to remove, you tell it what to keep: a meteor blazing across the sky, a satellite arc you want for effect, or any trail that adds rather than distracts. Everything else stays clean.
98.6%
Selective-mode classifier accuracy on held-out test data. In automatic mode Star never calls the classifier — the median merge alone removes streaks. Selective mode adds an ML decision tree that scores each outlier group when you need fine-grained control over what gets painted. Each labeled sequence you validate in Star App can be contributed to grow the training set — improving accuracy for everyone over time.
WHAT YOU GET
✈️
Batch-process an entire night's shoot unattended
Run star -w /path/to/sequence and walk away. The CLI processes every frame, writes a fully cleaned image sequence next to your originals, and saves a JSON config file you can re-open in the GUI to review or adjust. No babysitting required — come back when it's done.
🤖
Fully automatic, or bring back what you want to keep
Run the CLI and Star processes the whole sequence unattended — the median merge removes every streak without any classifier. Then open the result in Star App and switch to edit mode to restore anything the automatic pass discarded: a meteor you want in the final cut, a satellite trail kept for effect, or any group that shouldn't have been painted. Selective mode puts you in control of the exceptions, not the rule.
🖼
Frame-by-frame review in Star App
Load a processed sequence in the GUI, scrub through it, and tap any group to restore or repaint it. Star App shows every decision the automatic pass made — and lets you flip it with a single tap to recover meteors, intentional streaks, or anything else you want back in the final cut.
🌙
Designed for dark-sky work
Built by a night-sky photographer for night-sky photographers. The classifier was trained on real sequences from remote desert locations — tuned to distinguish faint satellites and dim prop planes from large stars, clouds, and terrain on the horizon.
☄️
Meteor-aware — restore what you want to keep
Meteor trails are classified similarly to airplanes and will often be removed automatically. Star App lets you restore any group you want to keep — so you can hand back the meteor that lit up frame 347 without re-running the whole sequence.
🎥
Fits any post-processing pipeline
Star operates on image sequences — it uses OpenCV internally, so most common formats work. 16-bit TIFFs from Lightroom, LRTimelapse, and Topaz Denoise are the typical handoff. Process with Star as your final step before encoding to video with ffmpeg or Resolve. Star App can also work directly on an already-rendered video: it decodes the video into a 16-bit image sequence, removes streaks, and re-encodes using the same codec — so no extra generation of encoding loss is introduced. This video round-trip is a Star App feature; the CLI works with image sequences only.

A typical workflow

  • Shoot overnight — capture your timelapse sequence as RAW or high-quality JPEG.
  • Develop in Lightroom + LRTimelapse — grade, deflicker, and export an image sequence.
  • Denoise (optional) — run Topaz Denoise AI or similar on the sequence.
  • Run star -w /path/to/sequence — Star processes every frame and writes the cleaned sequence alongside the originals.
  • Review in Star App — open the config JSON, scrub through, and correct any misclassified groups.
  • Encode to video — use Star App, ffmpeg, DaVinci Resolve, or star.pl to render the final timelapse.
